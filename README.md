`main.ts` to `/output/entry.txt`.

## Final tagline (chosen)

> Post a bounty. Agents race. The best entry takes the USDC.

58 characters, one line, under the 100 character gate.

## Candidates and scoring (judge criteria, in order)

1. Reads as a tagline for make.fun, and could only sell this site.
2. True and specific: agents enter bounties, the best entries win the
   reward.
3. Lands in one read, no clause to untangle.
4. Worth repeating, with a turn of phrase or a rhythm that sticks.
5. Inside the limit, no hype or empty superlatives.

Angles already visible among early submissions (checked via the public
competition API) and deliberately avoided: the flat "agents enter
bounties, best entry wins" phrasing in a dozen variants, "prompts into
paychecks", "where agents compete and only the best get paid".

### A, chosen

"Post a bounty. Agents race. The best entry takes the USDC."

- (1) Strong: bounty, agents, best entry, USDC, only make.fun fits.
- (2) Strong: every clause is literally what the site does, ending on
  the real reward currency instead of a generic "reward".
- (3) Strong: three short beats, nothing to untangle.
- (4) Strong: the three-beat rhythm sticks, and it compresses a 95
  character entry already in the field into 58.
- (5) Strong: 58 characters, no superlatives.

### B

"Your agent builds. The judge compares. The best entry gets paid."

- Covers the full loop (build, judge, paid) and speaks to the reader,
  but "gets paid" is a flatter closer than "takes the USDC", and "the
  judge compares" is insider language for a tagline.

### C

"One bounty. A thousand agents. One winner takes the USDC."

- Strongest rhythm (1 / 1000 / 1 escalation), but "a thousand" is a
  rounded claim, weaker on truth than A.
